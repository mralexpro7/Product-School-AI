Juno PM: Weekly Risk Brief

An AI assistant designed to help RocketShip PMs find the risks that need attention, with evidence for each claim and decisions left to the PM.

What I built

Five modules, one AI Associate PM

Each module added one piece. Together they define what the AI does, what it is allowed to do, and what good looks like.

Module 1
Skill file
Role

Juno is an AI Associate PM for RocketShip’s product team, designed to work with approved Slack, Jira and Notion sources. It acts as a risk watchdog. The PM owns decisions and follow-up actions.

Task

Turn scattered signals into a concise private brief with up to three risks or decisions that deserve PM attention. Cite the evidence, explain impact and suggest the next step. Mark unsupported or ambiguous details NEEDS CLARIFICATION. Never invent facts or draft external customer messages.

Module 2
Prototype
Prototype

https://alloy.app/flexspring/p/39e430d0-0880-4c95-8263-75dd839c38f4

What it demonstrates

The static Alloy prototype shows a Monday risk brief with three cards. Each card includes a finding, priority, impact, source excerpt and suggested next step. It demonstrates the proposed reading experience. The goal of finding the top issue within one minute still needs testing with PMs.

Debrief

Worked: the three-card layout makes risks easy to scan, and the billing example visibly flags an unsupported impact. Limitation: fictional, static examples and simulated View source buttons do not test live retrieval or prove time savings. Next: make sources open correctly, test the one-minute reading goal and run the quality checks before connecting real sources.

Module 3
AI PRD
Problem and user

RocketShip’s PMs face scattered signals and competing requests. Juno focuses on a Monday brief with up to three supported risks, ranked using product strategy. The PRD defines six parts around the model: approved context, read/draft tools, a bounded reasoning loop, memory rules, permissions and verification. Course mode uses five fixed documents covering signals, strategy, capacity, roadmap and pipeline. Live access remains proposed.

Permissions

Read approved sources only within the PM’s and viewer’s access. Draft a private brief automatically. Write and send are blocked, including Jira edits and stakeholder messages. The PM handles external communications, commitments and legal interpretation. Raw excerpts expire at session end or 24 hours, whichever comes first. Approved briefs last 90 days with PM approval. Before showing a finding, check its source excerpt and factual support. These are specified controls, not implemented connectors.

Module 4
Agent control panel
What it is doing

Proposed single-agent workflow: retrieve signals, check supporting evidence, rank verified risks and draft the brief. Module 4 extends Module 3’s PM-started brief to weekday monitoring at 09:00 in a PM-approved time zone. Monday produces the weekly brief. Other days refresh the private risk view. Scheduling and live connectors are not implemented in the current prototype.

Rules of engagement

The PM approves sources, audience, schedule and budget before activation. Each run allows at most 6 model calls, 12 reads and 90 seconds, with proposed model spend capped at $0.25. Evidence quality controls whether an item becomes a verified card or an unranked clarification question. Missing sources or missed runs must show an INCOMPLETE warning. Never hide gaps or fill space with general status updates. Juno cannot send messages or change records. Human handoff is a private note. At up to 23 scheduled runs, the model-only budget is $5.75 per team per month, excluding other costs.

Module 5
Human eval rubric
What I score

Score the Weekly Risk Brief on five questions: are its claims supported, does it expose uncertainty and missing sources, does it protect privacy and human control, are its priorities defensible, and can the PM understand the issue and next decision within one minute? Proposed review: me and a second informed reviewer score five test briefs weekly. Calibrate on three shared examples and resolve disagreements using the original evidence.

Pass bar

Each output must pass all three yes/no checks and score at least 4/5 on prioritization and usefulness. At least 80% of reviewed outputs must pass, with 100% factual citation coverage and zero fabricated facts or prohibited actions across the test set. Any invented claim, hidden coverage gap, unauthorized disclosure, unsafe action or unfair treatment blocks the affected use. Before release, run the 10 Module 3 test cases plus Module 4 failure cases and fairness pairs. At least 4 of 5 PM reviewers must identify the top issue and next decision within 60 seconds. These are proposed gates. No eval results are claimed.

Module 6

How I’ll know it’s working

The goal the AI is meant to move, where it stands today, and the result that would make me stop.

The goal

Reduce the PM time needed to produce a usable weekly risk brief while meeting the Module 5 quality bar.

Baseline

Planning assumption: 60 minutes per manual brief. This has not been measured. Before the pilot, time manual preparation on comparable course cases using the same sources, scope and quality standard. Include gathering evidence, checking it and writing the brief. Record the measured baseline before reviewing Juno’s results.

Target and review date

Proposed four-session pilot from Oct 19 to Nov 9, reviewed Nov 16, 2026. Target: average total PM time of 30 minutes or less AND at least 50% below the measured manual baseline. Include setup allocated across sessions, prompting, checking, edits and manual recovery. Compare matched cases using the same sources and quality standard, and vary task order to reduce practice effects. If readiness slips, reset dates before starting. The one-minute reading goal is separate.

Usage metric

Reliance rate = briefs used without major rewriting / all four planned weekly brief tasks. Target: at least 3 of 4 (75%). Major rewriting changes the selected risks, their priorities or recommendations. Normal source checking still applies. Failed or incomplete runs needing manual replacement count as not used. Record them separately so fast partial drafts cannot look like success.

Pattern I’ll watch for first

Watch first for checking taking too long. Log PM minutes spent verifying sources on every task. If total preparation stays above 30 minutes, improve working source links and excerpts, then retest. Track Module 5 pass rate and serious failures alongside time. Log model spend per run against the proposed $0.25 cap. Full task cost also includes PM review time and connector/hosting costs.

Stop line

Expand testing only if average total PM time meets both time targets, at least 3/4 briefs are used without major rewriting, and Module 5 gates pass. Improve and retest if Juno saves some time but misses a time, usage or quality target. Stop this use if time equals or exceeds the measured manual baseline (60 minutes under the current assumption). Pause immediately for any Module 5 deal-breaker. Four sessions guide further testing and do not prove broad business impact.

Reflection

What I learned

Where it was hardest, what I’ll keep doing, and the moment my thinking changed.

Friction

The hardest parts were choosing useful AI evaluation metrics, deciding how many times the model should loop, and understanding memory versus context. I found it difficult to turn those technical ideas into clear product decisions I could explain and test.

Key learnings

I will define the task and quality checks before adding automation. I will separate the context supplied for a run from information deliberately kept in memory, and set clear limits on retries and model calls. I will measure the human time spent checking the answer as part of the value of the product.

Aha moment

Working through the harness and evaluation requirements helped me see that my role includes deciding when the AI should stop and ask for help. A polished answer is only useful when its evidence is clear and it improves the PM’s work.
